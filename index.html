Tips

  • Numbers are read in the direction set at the top (right to left by default, as Niveans write). Mirrored glyphs are recognised too.
  • Glyphs should be upright and separated by a little space; one number per line. A wide gap starts a new number.
  • If other things in the picture get in the way, use Select area and drag a box around the numerals.
  • Amber boxes are guesses it is unsure about. Tap a digit below the picture to correct it (the list shows the best and second guess).
  • A small dot low on the line is read as a point. Ordinary text lines are skipped.

About the Niveans' system

The Niveans of the planet Niveus have thirteen fingers, so they count in base 13 (tridecimal). Digits ten, eleven and twelve are written here as A, B and C.

Nivean numbers are written right to left: the most significant digit is on the right. A date reading "NIVEUS 20099AE" shows the glyphs 1 C 1 9 from left to right, which read from the right are 91C1 in base 13 = 9·2197 + 1·169 + 12·13 + 1 = 20099. The glyphs are also mirrored in right-to-left writing: there the C has its corner squares top-left, top-right and bottom-left (the wiki chart has bottom-right), and the 2's slash runs the other way. With the switch at the top on RTL, this app writes numbers right to left with mirrored glyphs; LTR uses the chart's forms in European order. The image reader accepts both. Base-13 text such as 91C1 always stays in the usual left-to-right notation.

Assumptions made here

  • Procul = 9.9 m by default. "A bit less than 10 m" and "2197 procul ≈ 22 km" pull in different directions (22 km ÷ 2197 ≈ 10.01 m), so 9.9 m is a compromise. You can change it under Units → Assumptions.
  • Nivean day = 11 h by default. The wiki also says an aeta (182 days) is about 91 Earth days, which would mean 12-hour days. Editable too.
  • The radix point (the "decimal" point for fractions) has no known Nivean glyph, so a dot is used.
  • The 8 compass directions are not named on the wiki, so they are numbered 1–8 clockwise from the first.
